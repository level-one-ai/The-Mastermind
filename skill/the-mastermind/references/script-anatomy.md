# Script anatomy

A Mastermind script is a set of sections in a fixed order. The IDs never change, because the Call Desk shows them and the learning loop compares scripts section by section.

| # | id | Label in the desk | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `prep` | Before you dial | Who, why them, what you want, best time, TPS/CTPS reminder, the one fact to have in your head | 4 to 6 short lines, not read out |
| 2 | `gatekeeper` | If someone else answers | Get through or get the owner's name and best time | 2 to 4 lines |
| 3 | `opener` | Opener | Name, company, honest permission ask. Buys the next 30 seconds | ≤ 30 seconds aloud, about 40 to 60 words |
| 4 | `reason` | Why I'm calling | The real reason for this company, plus one pain line they'd say themselves | 2 to 3 sentences |
| 5 | `questions` | Questions | Two or three Mom Test questions about what happened last time, not what they'd do | 2 to 3 questions plus what to listen for |
| 6 | `bridge` | If it's a problem for them | One or two sentences linking their answer to what Dean builds, in outcomes | ≤ 3 sentences |
| 7 | `ask` | The ask | One ask, two specific times, a no-pressure line | 2 to 3 sentences |
| 8 | `objections` | If they push back | The four likeliest brush-offs for this lead, plus "how much is it?" and "don't call me again" | 6 pairs, 1 to 3 sentences each |
| 9 | `voicemail` | Voicemail | Name, one reason, number twice | ≤ 25 seconds |
| 10 | `booked` | When they say yes | Confirm time, place, their mobile, what happens next | 2 to 3 lines |
| 11 | `followup` | Text after the call | The SMS or email Dean sends within 5 minutes (booked, voicemail or "send me info") | ≤ 50 words |

## Writing conventions the desk understands

- Plain spoken text. One paragraph per breath.
- Stage directions in underscores: `_(pause, let them answer)_`. The desk shows them small and grey so Dean doesn't read them out.
- Objections as pairs, one per line:
  `They: We already have someone for that.`
  `You: Good, most people do. ...`
- Questions start with `Q:`. What to listen for starts with `Listen for:`.

## The four-beat opener (default)

1. **Who** (3 to 5s): "Hi Gary, it's Dean Finlayson from Level One." Full name, company, no "how are you today".
2. **Permission** (3 to 5s): "This is a cold call, so I'll be quick. Can I have 30 seconds to say why I'm calling, and you tell me if it's worth carrying on?" Name the interruption, never apologise for it.
3. **Reason** (5s): "The reason I'm calling is..." If Dean can't finish that sentence for this company, the lead isn't ready.
4. **Then stop.** The `reason` section continues only after they say go on.

Variants change exactly one of these. See `openers.md`.

## The pain line

One sentence the owner would say out loud about their own week.

- Verified signal: state it plainly. "I saw on your Google page a couple of folk said they couldn't get through on the phone."
- No signal: hedge it as what peers say. "Most plumbers I speak to tell me the phone goes when they're under a sink and that's the job gone. I'm guessing that happens to you too, but you'd know better."
- Never the solution, never jargon, never a feature.

## When the dad story earns its place

Only in `bridge` or an objection answer, only one sentence, and only when the owner has said the business eats their evenings or weekends: "That's actually why I started this. Watched my dad do his paperwork at the kitchen table every night." Never in the opener.

## Proof without clients

Dean can't name clients and Level One is new. Honest proof that works on the phone:
- **Local**: "I'm in Bonnyrigg/Edinburgh, I can come and see you."
- **Builder**: "I build it myself, you deal with me, not a call centre."
- **Low risk**: "Fifteen minutes, I'll show you what it would look like for your business. If it's not for you, no bother."

Never invent numbers ("saves 10 hours a week") unless the Company Book gives a figure Dean can stand behind.
