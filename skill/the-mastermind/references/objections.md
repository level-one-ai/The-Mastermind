# Objections

## Diagnose first

The words aren't always the objection.

| Type | Signs | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Reflex brush-off | First 30 seconds, generic: "not interested", "send me something", "busy" | Agree, one question, at most one turn-around |
| Smokescreen | Vague, shifts when probed | "Can I ask what's behind that?" then re-diagnose |
| Real objection | Specific, tied to facts: "my nephew does our website" | Answer honestly, one proof point, hand back a question |
| Dead | No phone, retiring, "don't call me again" | Polite exit, log it, stop |

Pause a beat before answering. The instinct to defend is almost always wrong.

## The cold-call shape

**Ledge, disarm, ask.** A calm neutral line, agree with them, one question that checks whether the problem is real. Never argue with a reflex: arguing turns it into a real objection. **Never more than two tries.** Then: "No bother at all, thanks for your time."

## Library (rewrite in the lead's own world every time)

They: I'm not interested.
You: Fair enough, most people aren't until they know why I'm calling. Quick one before I go: when the phone rings and you're on a job, does that call get answered?

They: Send me an email.
You: Happy to. So I don't send you something you'll bin, is missing calls actually a thing for you, or are you on top of it?
_(Their answer qualifies them either way. Log as Asked for info if they won't say.)_

They: I'm busy.
You: No problem at all. Is first thing tomorrow better, or end of the day?
_(Always two specific times.)_

They: We've already got someone / my nephew does our website.
You: Good, I'd be surprised if you didn't. I'm not a website guy. Out of interest, when someone rings at 7 at night, what happens?

They: How much is it?
You: Depends what you need. It's a one-off build and then a small monthly. I'd rather show you first than throw a number at you. Fifteen minutes, Thursday or Friday?

They: Is this one of those AI things?
You: It's just your phone and your paperwork doing more of the work for you. I build it myself, here in Edinburgh. Easier to show you than explain.

They: How did you get my number?
You: It's on your website / Google page. I'm local and I'm ringing a handful of businesses round here. Happy to take you off my list if you'd rather.

They: We're too small for that.
You: That's who I build it for. Most of the folk I speak to are one or two vans. The bigger firms have office staff for this; you've got you.

They: Not right now / call me in the new year.
You: Will do. So I'm not guessing, is the second week of January alright? I'll give you a ring Tuesday morning.
_(Log as Callback with the date.)_

They: Don't call me again.
You: No problem, I'll take you off. Sorry for the interruption, all the best.
_(Log as Do not call. Never a second line.)_

## Banned

- Feel-felt-found ("others felt the same, they found...") when the others are invented.
- Fake urgency, fake scarcity, invented social proof.
- Trashing whoever they use now.
- A rebuttal quota. A no after two tries is a no.

## Learning

Every lost lead logs a reason. When one reason tops the scoreboard (for example "Already have someone" 40% of losses), the next scripts in that niche move that objection to the top of the block and the analyst proposes a better answer as a challenger.
