# Signals: the reason to call

An angle is: **signal** (fact, source, date) → **inference** (so they probably face X) → **hook** (one spoken line) → **ask**. Label each signal: verified / single-source / guessed, and how old.

## Where to look (fast first)

| Signal | Where | Usually means |
|---|---|---|
| Reviews mentioning phones, slow replies, no-shows | Google, Facebook | Calls and enquiries leaking. The strongest signal Dean can find |
| Owner replies to reviews (or doesn't) | Google | Owner's first name; how stretched they are |
| "Call us" as the only contact, no form or booking | Website | Everything goes through one phone |
| Hiring an office admin / apprentice | Indeed, Facebook, website | Admin pain right now |
| New van, second premises, expanding area | Facebook posts | Growing faster than the admin |
| Opening hours shorter than when customers ring | Google | Out-of-hours enquiries lost |
| Website looks untouched for years | Website | Not their priority; don't lead with it |

## The "so what?" test

Read the signal as the owner: "so what does that mean for me?" If the honest answer is "nothing", drop it. "Congratulations on 10 years" fails. "Two reviews say they couldn't get through" passes.

## Anti-fabrication

- Never invent a review, a name, a date or a number.
- If the owner's name isn't public, the opener asks for "the owner" or uses no name. Never guess a name.
- Never use personal details (family, health, home address) even if public. Business-visible signals only.
- Thin research: say so in `prep` and use the hedged niche pain line.
