# Gatekeepers, voicemail and the text after

## Gatekeeper

Small businesses: the "gatekeeper" is usually the owner's partner, a receptionist or an apprentice. Be straight with them.

- Confident: "Hi, it's Dean Finlayson, is Gary about?" Said like you're expected, no "I was wondering if".
- Ask for help: "I'm trying to get Gary about how calls get answered when he's out on jobs. Is he the right person, or is that someone else?"
- Blocked: "No bother. When's the best time to catch him?" Get a name and a time. Write the gatekeeper's name in the call note; next time use it.
- Never lie about who you are or why you're calling.

Trades owners are easiest to reach 7:30 to 8:30 before the first job, or 5 to 6:30 after. Shops and salons: mid-morning or mid-afternoon, never at opening. Offices: 8 to 9:30 or 4 to 5:30. Tuesday to Thursday are best.

## Voicemail

Under 25 seconds. One idea. Number said slowly, twice.

> Hi Gary, it's Dean Finlayson from Level One in Edinburgh. I'm calling about the calls you miss when you're on a job. I'll drop you a text with what I mean. It's Dean, 07... I'll say that again, 07...

- Leave one on the 1st and 3rd attempt, skip the 2nd.
- Change the message each time.
- Send the text or email within 5 minutes.
- Never "just calling to introduce myself".

## Text after the call (followup section)

Within 5 minutes, signed "Dean, Level One".

- **Booked**: confirm day, time, place. "Thanks Gary, see you Thursday 10am at the yard. Dean, Level One"
- **Voicemail**: "Hi Gary, Dean here, left you a voicemail. Calls you miss on jobs, I build a thing so every one gets a text back straight away. Worth 15 mins? Dean, Level One"
- **Asked for info**: one short paragraph answering the thing they said mattered, plus one question.

Dean's mobile number goes in from the Company Book or from Dean. If it's not known, ask him once and write it into `library/voice/voice-profile.md` under "Details". Never leave a placeholder.
