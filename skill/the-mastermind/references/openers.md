# Openers

The opener's only job is to earn the next 30 seconds. It ends when the owner engages or brushes off.

## The menu (ranked for Dean: solo, local owners, new to the phone)

1. **Permission-based** (default). "This is a cold call, can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and you decide if it's worth carrying on?" In Gong's 2024 study this and context-first tied as the best openers (about 11% of connected calls to a held meeting). Low rehearsal, fails softly.
2. **Direct reason-for-call**. "The reason I'm calling is..." Cheapest to learn and pairs with everything. Promote it to first for owner-operators, re-attempts and warm-ish leads (they filled in a form, Dean met them somewhere). On a re-attempt, say so honestly: "I tried you on Tuesday."
3. **Context-first**. Lead with the verified signal: "I was looking at your Google page, you've got 80-odd five-star reviews and two of the recent ones say they couldn't get through on the phone." Tied for best value but needs a real, specific signal. Only when research found one.
4. **Pattern interrupt**. "You don't know me and this is a cold call. Want to hang up or give me 30 seconds?" No dataset isolates it. Use for a challenger only.

**Deleted, not ranked:** "How are you today?", "How have you been?" (fake familiarity to a stranger), "Did I catch you at a bad time?" (invites the yes), "Sorry to bother you" (apologising signals you shouldn't be there), "Have you heard of Level One?" (centres Dean, not them).

## Variants

A challenger changes **one** thing against the champion: the opener pattern, or the pain wording, or the ask. Change two and the result teaches nothing. Write what changed in the script's `variable` field.

## Delivery notes to put in `prep`

- Slow down on your name. Rushing sounds scared.
- Drop your voice at the end of sentences.
- After the permission question, stop. Whoever talks next is them.
- Stand up for call blocks.
- Successful cold calls aren't interrogations: one confident burst for the opener, then short quick turns.

## Numbers to judge an opener by

- Connect to conversation (they talk past the opener). Benchmark about 65% (Cognism 2025).
- Meetings per conversation. Industry average about 2.7%; good teams 6 to 11%. Dean's desk tracks booked vs lost, which is stricter: a template booking 1 in 10 decided leads is doing well.

Never compare two openers on fewer than 10 decided leads each.
